What do I need to give?

A solute: a short id, its free-solution diffusion coefficient D = D0 + DT·T (m² yr⁻¹; from 10⁻⁶ cm² s⁻¹ multiply by 0.0031558) and its bottom-water concentration (µmol kg⁻¹). It diffuses with the tortuosity correction, is buried with the porewater and exchanged across the boundary layer and by bio-irrigation, like the built-in solutes. Boudreau (1997): O2 0.0316 + 0.00143 T; Ca²⁺ 0.0118 + 0.00053 T; Ba²⁺ 0.0128 + 0.00056 T.

A solid: its rain to the seafloor (mol m⁻² yr⁻¹) and, unless it is a trace, its molar mass (g mol⁻¹), which adds to the burial velocity. Solids are mixed by bioturbation and buried.

A reaction: its rate law and stoichiometry. The rate R is in mol per m³ of its reference phase (that of its first reactant) per year; each species changes at coefficient × R, converted between porewater and solid volumes automatically. Inside the model solutes are in mol m⁻³ of porewater (1 µmol kg⁻¹ ≈ 1.03 × 10⁻³ mol m⁻³) and solids in mol m⁻³ of solid. If a reaction releases or consumes acid, include TA in its stoichiometry.

A sediment column, solved to steady state

RADI computes porewater and solid profiles, reaction rates and the fluxes a sediment exchanges with the seawater above it, for organic carbon and calcite raining onto the seafloor.

  1. Pick a station on the left — it fills in every input and gives the solver a converged starting state.
  2. Change anything: water depth, temperature, bottom-water chemistry, the rain of organic carbon or calcite, porosity…
  3. Press Solve. From a station or your last run it takes a second or two; keep a run to compare scenarios side by side.
